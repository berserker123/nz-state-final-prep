Pain assessment phrases for nurses

Plain-English questions for asking about pain: where, when, what it feels like, how bad, what helps, and what it stops the person doing. Practise saying them clearly and kindly.

Situation

You are a registered nurse on a ward, in a clinic or at home. A patient says they are sore or uncomfortable, or you are doing an assessment, and you need to ask about pain in words the person understands. People describe pain in many ways and some do not like to complain, so ask open questions first and then use a pain tool that your workplace has chosen. The HQSC pain guides cited here (S42, S43) were written for aged residential care and frailty care; they are used on this page only as sources for general communication prompts. This page teaches the language only. It does not tell you what to give or when; medicines and doses follow the prescription, the New Zealand Formulary (NZF) and local policy.

Phrase cards

English phrase中文When to useNote
Hello, I'm [your name], your nurse today. How are you feeling? Are you in any pain or discomfort?您好，我是 [姓名]，今天负责您的护士。您感觉怎么样？有没有疼痛或不舒服？Opening the conversation.Ask about 'pain or discomfort' together. Some people do not use the word pain.
Can you show me where it hurts?您能指给我看哪里痛吗？Finding the site.Pointing can help when words are hard. Ask about the site and whether it spreads (the HQSC aged residential care pain guide uses prompts such as provokes, quality, radiation, severity and time).
Does the pain move anywhere else, or stay in one place?疼痛会放射到别的地方，还是一直在同一个位置？Checking whether pain spreads.Record what the patient says in their own words.
When did it start? Did it come on suddenly or slowly?是什么时候开始的？是突然出现还是慢慢出现的？Asking about onset.Two short questions are easier to follow than one long one.
What does it feel like? For example sharp, dull, burning, aching or throbbing.是什么样的痛？比如刺痛、钝痛、烧灼痛、酸痛或搏动样痛。Asking about the type of pain.Offer a few words as examples, but let the patient use their own.
On a scale from 0 to 10, where 0 is no pain and 10 is the worst pain you can imagine, what number is it right now?如果 0 分是完全不痛，10 分是您能想象的最痛，您现在是几分？Rating the pain with a numbers scale.Say what 0 and 10 mean every time. Use the scale or tool your workplace has chosen, and use the same one each time so scores can be compared.
What makes it better? What makes it worse?做什么会让它好一点？做什么会让它更痛？Asking about triggers and relief.Write down what the patient tells you, in their own words.
Does the pain stop you from sleeping, moving, eating or doing things you need to do?疼痛有没有影响您睡觉、活动、吃饭或做您需要做的事？Asking about the effect on daily life.This shows how much pain matters to the person, not only the number.
Have you had anything for the pain? Did it help?您用过什么止痛的方法或药吗？有用吗？Asking what has been tried.Includes medicines from the pharmacy, heat, rest and traditional remedies. Do not judge. Medicines are given only as prescribed and in line with local policy.
Is there anything else you feel with the pain, like feeling sick, sweaty or short of breath?疼痛时您还有别的不舒服吗，比如恶心、出汗或气短？Checking other symptoms.If the patient describes new or worrying symptoms, tell the nurse in charge or the doctor straight away, following your escalation pathway.
Would you like a support person with you? And would an interpreter help us talk this through?您希望有人陪着您吗？另外，请一位口译员帮我们沟通，会不会更方便？Offering support with communication.Right 5 of the HDC Code covers effective communication, including a competent interpreter where necessary and reasonably practicable. A support person is welcome but should not replace a competent interpreter. Follow your service's interpreter policy.
Some people find it hard to say they are sore. Please tell me anytime, even if it seems small.有些人不太好意思说自己痛。任何时候都可以告诉我，哪怕觉得是小事。Inviting honest answers.Some people keep pain to themselves, for example because they are private, do not want to be a burden, or feel whakamā (shame, embarrassment). Culture can affect how pain is felt and shown (HQSC frailty care guide). Ask openly and do not assume.
I'll check on you again in a little while to see how you're feeling.过一会儿我会再来看您，问问您现在感觉怎么样。After you act, or when you leave.Pain is reassessed after any action, and the result is recorded accurately and on time.
Just so I know I explained it clearly, can you tell me in your own words what we're going to do?为了确认我讲清楚了，您能用自己的话告诉我，我们接下来要怎么做吗？Checking understanding of a plan.This is the 'check you were clear' step in the HQSC health literacy guide. Make it about your explanation, not the patient's ability.

Model dialogue

  • Nurse: Hello Mrs Wong, I'm Tama, your nurse this afternoon. Are you in any pain or discomfort?

    Wong 太太，您好，我是 Tama，今天下午负责您的护士。您有没有疼痛或不舒服？

  • Patient: Yes, my tummy is sore.

    有，我肚子痛。

  • Nurse: I'm sorry to hear that. Can you show me where it hurts?

    让您受苦了。您能指给我看哪里痛吗？

  • Patient: Here, low down on the right side.

    这里，右下腹。

  • Nurse: When did it start, and what does it feel like? Is it sharp, dull or aching?

    是什么时候开始的？是什么样的痛？是刺痛、钝痛还是酸痛？

  • Patient: It started after lunch. It's a dull ache, but sharper when I move.

    午饭后开始的。是钝痛，动的时候更尖锐一些。

  • Nurse: Thank you. On a scale from 0 to 10, where 0 is no pain and 10 is the worst you can imagine, what number is it now?

    谢谢。如果 0 分是完全不痛，10 分是您能想象的最痛，您现在是几分？

  • Patient: About 6.

    大概 6 分。

  • Nurse: Okay, 6 out of 10. I'll write that down and let the nurse in charge know. I'll come back soon to see how you're feeling. Can you tell me what you'll do if it gets worse before I'm back?

    好的，10 分里的 6 分。我会记下来，并告诉当班负责护士。我很快会再来看您，看看您感觉怎么样。如果在我回来前更痛了，您打算怎么做？

  • Patient: I'll press the call bell.

    我会按呼叫铃。
