Draft, pending RN review. One-page reference for the four calculation types used in Part A exam practice.

Reference

The calculation steps below are the same ones the Part A question generator uses, with the same rounding. Keep full precision until the final step, then round once.

Calculation Formula Variables Rounding
Metric unit conversion y = x × k x = starting amount; k = 1000 or 1/1000 (mcg↔mg, mg↔g, mL↔L) Exact; no rounding
Oral liquid dose volume v = D × V ÷ S D = prescribed dose (mg); S = stock strength (mg); V = stock volume (mL); v = volume to give (mL) 1 decimal place at the final step (half-up)
Weight-based dose volume v = W × r ÷ (N × C) W = weight (kg); r = ordered rate (mg/kg/day); N = doses per day; C = concentration (mg/mL); v = volume per dose (mL) 1 decimal place at the final step (half-up)
Infusion pump rate R = V ÷ t V = total volume (mL); t = time (hours); R = pump rate (mL/h) Part A items use volumes that divide exactly, so the rate is a whole number: no rounding step

Not included yet: tablet counts, drops per minute and New Zealand safe-writing rules for doses. They need a registered primary source first.

Worked examples

All numbers are for arithmetic practice only. They are not dosing advice.

Metric unit conversion

Example 1. Convert 75 mcg to mg.

  • Working: 75 × 1 ÷ 1000 = 0.075 mg
  • Answer: 0.075 mg

Example 2. Convert 4 g to mg.

  • Working: 4 × 1000 = 4000 mg
  • Answer: 4000 mg

Oral liquid dose volume

Example 1. Stock is paracetamol oral suspension 120 mg in 5 mL (strength as listed in the Junior Parapaed data sheet). The dose of 180 mg is chosen for the arithmetic only.

  • Working: 180 × 5 ÷ 120 = 7.5 → 7.5 mL
  • Answer: 7.5 mL

Example 2. Same stock strength, dose 150 mg. This one shows half-up rounding at the final step.

  • Working: 150 × 5 ÷ 120 = 6.25 → 6.3 mL
  • Answer: 6.3 mL

Weight-based dose volume

Example 1. Fictional medicine: 24 mg/mL, ordered 60 mg/kg/day in 4 equal doses, child weighs 12 kg. Rates here are made up for the arithmetic.

  • Working: 12 × 60 ÷ (4 × 24) = 720 ÷ 96 = 7.5 → 7.5 mL
  • Answer: 7.5 mL

Infusion pump rate

Example 1. Infuse 1000 mL of IV fluid over 8 hours by volumetric pump.

  • Working: 1000 ÷ 8 = 125 → 125 mL/h
  • Answer: 125 mL/h

Example 2. Infuse 600 mL of IV fluid over 4 hours by volumetric pump.

  • Working: 600 ÷ 4 = 150 → 150 mL/h
  • Answer: 150 mL/h
