HPCA Act 2003: NZ nursing quick reference

The Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act 2003 aims to protect public health and safety by ensuring health practitioners are competent and fit to practise (s 3). This page covers titles, scope of practice, restricted activities and notification duties.

Key provisions

ProvisionPlain-English point中文要点What the nurse does
s 3: purposeThe principal purpose is to protect the health and safety of the public by providing mechanisms to ensure that health practitioners are competent and fit to practise. It does this through a common accountability regime, a scope of practice for each practitioner, and systems to stop practice outside scope.首要目的是通过确保健康从业者胜任并适合执业的机制来保护公众健康和安全，办法包括统一的问责制度、为每位从业者确定执业范围，以及防止超范围执业的系统。Think of the Act as public protection: competence and fitness to practise, not a list of clinical procedures.
s 7: protected titlesA person may only use names, titles or descriptions stating or implying they are a health practitioner of a particular kind if they are registered and qualified to be registered as that kind. Claiming to practise that profession also needs a current practising certificate. The offence carries a fine of up to $10,000. Section 7(4) allows stating willingness to practise when seeking employment if the person would be qualified to be registered and hold a current practising certificate on obtaining the job.只有已注册且有资格注册的人，才可使用表明或暗示自己是某类健康从业者的名称、头衔或描述；声称执业还须持有有效的执业证书。违反者可处最高 1 万新西兰元罚款。s 7(4) 允许求职时表示愿意执业，前提是该人获得工作后将有资格注册并持有有效执业证书。Use a protected title only when you are registered (and qualified to be registered), and claim to practise only when you also hold a current practising certificate; describe your status accurately on forms and CVs.
s 8: scope of practiceA health practitioner who practises their registered profession must hold a current practising certificate, and must perform a health service only if their scope of practice permits it and in line with any conditions. Three exceptions apply (s 8(3)): an emergency; part of a course of training or instruction; or an examination, assessment, or competence review required or ordered by the responsible authority.执业的注册从业者须持有有效执业证书，且只有在其执业范围允许并遵守相关条件时才可提供健康服务；仅限三项例外：紧急情况、作为培训或教学课程的一部分，以及责任机构要求或命令的考试、评估或能力审查。Know your scope and conditions and say so when asked to do something outside them.
s 9: restricted activitiesAn Order in Council can declare an activity that is part of a health service a restricted activity. Nobody may perform, or say they are willing to perform, a restricted activity unless they are a health practitioner whose scope permits it. Exceptions apply (s 9(5)): in an emergency; during training or instruction under the control of a health practitioner whose scope permits that activity; or during an examination, assessment, or competence review required or ordered by the responsible authority. The offence carries a fine of up to $30,000.可通过总督行政会议令（Order in Council）将某项属于健康服务的活动宣布为受限活动；除非是执业范围允许的健康从业者，否则任何人不得实施或表示愿意实施该活动。实施受限活动的例外包括：紧急情况、在执业范围允许实施该活动的健康从业者控制下进行的培训或教学，以及责任机构要求或命令的考试、评估或能力审查。违反者可处最高 3 万新西兰元罚款。Check whether an activity is restricted and whether your scope permits it before doing it or delegating it.
s 34(1): notice about below-standard competenceA health practitioner who has reason to believe another practitioner may pose a risk of harm to the public by practising below the required standard of competence may give the Registrar written notice of the reasons. The wording is “may”.如果从业者有理由相信另一位从业者因低于要求的能力标准执业而可能对公众造成伤害，可以向注册官发出书面通知说明理由；条文用词是“可以”（may）。Know the difference between permission to notify (s 34(1)) and the duty in s 45; raise concerns through your workplace and professional processes.
s 34(3): resignation or dismissal for competence reasonsWhen an employee health practitioner resigns or is dismissed for reasons relating to competence, the person who employed them immediately before must promptly give the Registrar written notice of the reasons.受雇的健康从业者因与能力有关的原因辞职或被解雇时，其紧邻之前的雇主须及时向注册官发出书面通知说明原因。If you manage staff, know that this duty falls on the employer.
s 34(4) and s 45(6): protection for noticesNo civil or disciplinary proceedings lie against a person for a notice given under the section unless the person acted in bad faith.依本条发出通知的人不会因此面临民事或纪律程序，除非其出于恶意。Do not delay a notice because you fear proceedings, as long as you act in good faith.
s 41: recertification programmesTo ensure practitioners remain competent, each authority may set or recognise recertification programmes. These can require, for example, passing examinations or assessments or completing a period of practical training.为确保从业者保持胜任，各责任机构可制定或认可再认证项目，可要求通过考试或评估、完成一段实践培训等。Complete the competence requirements set for your registration and keep your records.
s 45(1)-(3): inability due to a mental or physical conditionA person in charge of a health-service organisation, a health practitioner, an employer of health practitioners, or a medical officer of health who has reason to believe a practitioner is unable to perform the functions required for practice because of a mental or physical condition must promptly give the Registrar written notice of all the circumstances. Any other person may give written notice.医疗机构负责人、健康从业者、健康从业者的雇主或卫生医官，如有理由相信某从业者因身体或精神状况而无法履行执业所需职能，必须及时向注册官发出书面通知说明全部情况；其他任何人可以发出书面通知。Act on safety first, then follow your workplace and notification pathway promptly.
s 118(1)(i): standards set by the authorityAmong the authority's functions is setting standards of clinical competence, cultural competence (including competencies that enable effective and respectful interaction with Māori) and ethical conduct for practitioners of the profession.责任机构的职能之一是为本专业从业者制定临床能力、文化能力（包括能够与毛利人进行有效且尊重的互动的能力）和伦理行为标准。Read the profession's own competence and conduct standards; this Act gives the authority the power to set them.

Scenarios

  1. A graduate who has finished the degree but is not yet registered is preparing a job application. Section 7(1) means they may not describe themselves as a registered nurse until they are registered and qualified to be registered, and s 7(4) allows them to say they are willing to practise when seeking employment if they would be qualified to be registered and hold a current practising certificate on getting the job. They write their status accurately.
  2. A nurse is asked to take on a task that does not appear to be within their scope of practice or conditions. Section 8(2) means a health practitioner may perform a health service only if their scope permits it and in line with its conditions, with exceptions such as emergencies in s 8(3). The nurse checks scope and conditions and raises the question with the person asking.
  3. A manager dismisses an employed nurse for reasons relating to competence. Section 34(3) requires the person who employed the nurse immediately before the dismissal to promptly give the Registrar written notice of the reasons. The manager follows that duty promptly.

Common mistakes

  • Believing a nurse must always notify the Registrar about any colleague's competence concern: s 34(1) says a practitioner “may” give notice, while the duty in s 45(2) concerns inability due to a mental or physical condition.
  • Thinking the s 45 duty applies only to employers: s 45(1) also covers health practitioners, persons in charge of health-service organisations and medical officers of health.
  • Assuming an emergency lifts every limit: s 8(3)(a) and s 9(5)(a) contain specific emergency exceptions, so read them for what they actually allow.
  • Treating the Act as a clinical practice standard: s 3 is about protecting the public through competence and fitness mechanisms, and s 118(1)(i) gives each authority the function of setting standards.
  • Confusing a protected title with qualifications: s 7 requires being registered and qualified to be registered, and practising also requires a current practising certificate.
